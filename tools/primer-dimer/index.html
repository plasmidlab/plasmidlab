How this is calculated
The page lays one strand under the other, antiparallel, at every offset and looks for runs of consecutive Watson-Crick pairs: A with T and G with C. A self-dimer is a primer against a second copy of itself; a cross-dimer is primer 1 against primer 2. A hairpin is the primer folded back on itself, with a stem of at least three pairs around a loop of at least three bases. U is read as T. IUPAC codes such as N have no pairing energy, so a primer that contains one is not scored.
The ΔG is for the strongest contiguous run at that alignment, at 37 °C, from the SantaLucia 1998 unified nearest-neighbour parameters, the same table the PlasmidLab app uses for primer Tm. It adds up the two-base steps of the run, plus a term for each end and, for a run that is its own reverse complement, a small symmetry term. It is at the 1 M NaCl standard state, with no salt correction. A hairpin’s ΔG is its stem steps plus a penalty for the loop by its length, from SantaLucia and Hicks 2004. In the drawings, | marks the scored run and : marks other complementary bases at the same alignment, which are not counted.
Some things are left out: mismatches and bulges inside or beside the run, G·T pairs, the unpaired bases hanging off its ends, and the extra stability of some short hairpin loops. Programs that score those give different numbers, mostly more negative. On a dozen test dimers a program that does came out between 0 and 3.5 kcal/mol more negative than this page, and 1 to 1.6 kcal/mol more negative on hairpins. For a perfectly matched 20-mer duplex the two agreed within 0.1 kcal/mol. The verdicts leave room for that gap.
Everything runs in your browser. The sequences are not sent anywhere.


What to do about it
A common rule of thumb is to reject a dimer at a 3′ end below about −5 kcal/mol and one elsewhere below about −6. A 3′ end matters most because it is the end a polymerase extends. The fixes are usually small: shift the primer a few bases along the template, or trim a base or two off its 3′ end. The PCR primer design guide covers these and when a hot-start polymerase helps.
