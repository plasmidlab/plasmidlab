Gateway® cloning: what the BP and LR reactions do

Gateway® cloning moves a piece of DNA between plasmids without cutting or ligating anything. Once you know which att sites react with which, the two reaction names stop being jargon.

Recombination, not restriction

The reaction borrows the machinery phage lambda uses to enter and leave the E. coli chromosome. Two plasmids, each carrying a recombination site, exchange the DNA between their sites. No restriction enzyme, no ligase, and the insert never has to be cut out.

The two reactions

BP joins attB and attP and leaves attL and attR. An insert flanked by attB sites, usually a PCR product made with attB tails on the primers, plus a donor vector carrying attP, gives you an entry clone carrying attL sites.

LR joins attL and attR and leaves attB and attP. The entry clone plus a destination vector carrying attR gives the expression clone, with the insert back between attB sites. One entry clone can go into as many destination vectors as you like, which is the point of the system.

The numbers keep the direction

Sites come in numbered pairs. Site 1 reacts with site 1 and site 2 with site 2, so the insert can only land one way round. That is why attB1 goes on one primer and attB2 on the other, and why swapping them flips the insert.

ccdB keeps the background down

Donor and destination vectors carry the ccdB gene between their att sites. Its product poisons DNA gyrase and kills ordinary E. coli, so a cell that took up an unreacted vector does not grow, and only the recombined product forms colonies. Those vectors have to be propagated in a ccdB-resistant strain, which is why they arrive as a separate stock.

Watch the reading frame

An att site ends up between a vector-encoded tag and your insert, and it is translated as part of the fusion. For an N-terminal tag, the frame has to run through attB1 and into your coding sequence without a stop; for a C-terminal tag, your insert must not carry its own stop codon. Count the bases on the finished sequence rather than trusting the primer design, and check the translation on the product.
