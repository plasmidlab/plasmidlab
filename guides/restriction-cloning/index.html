Restriction cloning: how to choose two enzymes

Most failed restriction clones fail at the planning stage: an enzyme that also cuts the insert, two enzymes that will not work in one buffer, or a site too close to the end of a PCR product.

Two enzymes, not one

Cutting the vector with two different enzymes that leave incompatible ends does two jobs at once. The insert can only go in one way round, and the vector cannot close on itself without an insert.

1. List what cuts the vector once

Open the vector and choose Analyze > Use Enzyme Set > 6 bp+ enzymes that cut once. The map then shows only single cutters, and the ones in the multiple cloning site are the candidates.

In pUC19 (NCBI L09137) the MCS single cutters run in this order, each cutting the top strand after the base shown: EcoRI 396, SacI 406, Acc65I 408, KpnI 412, XmaI 412, SmaI 414, BamHI 417, XbaI 423, SalI 429, PstI 439, SphI 445, HindIII 447. Any two of them that leave different ends will do, as long as they leave the fragment you want between them.

2. Check the insert

Open the insert and choose Analyze > Enzymes That Do Not Cut. Your two candidates must appear in that list. This is the step people skip, and an enzyme that cuts once inside the insert ruins the ligation quietly.

3. Check they share a buffer

Two enzymes in one tube need a buffer both are active in. Suppliers publish the activity of each enzyme in each of their buffers, and their double-digest tools answer it directly, such as NEBcloner for NEB enzymes. If no buffer suits both, digest with one, clean up, then digest with the other.

4. Leave room at the ends

Most enzymes cut poorly right at the end of a linear fragment, so a PCR product that carries a restriction site in its primer needs a few extra bases 5′ of the site. How many depends on the enzyme, and suppliers publish a table per enzyme, such as NEB's cleavage close to the end of DNA fragments. Six extra bases is a common safe default when you have no number for your enzyme.

5. Ligate, then check

A molar ratio of about 3:1 insert to vector is the usual starting point for a single insert. If both vector ends are compatible with each other, dephosphorylate the vector so it cannot re-close. Gel-purify the cut vector when the removed piece is small enough to carry over.

Confirm the colonies with a diagnostic digest before sequencing. Analyze > Simulate a Gel… predicts the fragment sizes for a digest of the expected plasmid, which tells you in advance whether the bands will be far enough apart to read.

Build it in PlasmidLab

Clone > Cut and Paste > Insert One Fragment… takes the insert from one file into the cut vector and makes the product. Clone > Ligate Fragments… joins fragments you have already cut, and Clone > Cut and Paste > Destroy a Site… removes a site you do not want.
