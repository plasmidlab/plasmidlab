How to Design a Gibson Assembly in PlasmidLab

Design a Gibson assembly from your own sequences, inspect the proposed primers and junctions, and create a predicted product in PlasmidLab. The manual cloning tools work offline and do not require an AI agent.

1. Open your sequences

Open the vector and the source files for your fragments with File > Open. You can also drag them into the app. The file-opening guide covers supported formats.

2. Choose the assembly and pieces

Choose Clone > Gibson Assembly…. Select a mode for one insert, multiple inserts, or assembly of multiple fragments. Choose the source file for each piece, the sequence range and orientation as needed. For a circular vector, select the enzymes that open it and the region to replace.

The assembly window supports up to 10 piece slots, including the vector when one is used. Each Gibson fragment must be at least 20 bases long.

3. Review primers and the predicted product

Use the Primers tab to inspect the designed primers, overlap junctions and any warnings. Use the Product tab to review the predicted construct. When the design is ready, choose a file name and click Assemble.

Inspect the product's sequence, junctions, feature orientation and expected reading frames before ordering primers or performing the experiment. A computational design is not experimental validation; check primer specificity and your assembly reagent's requirements independently.

Try a built-in example

Choose Help > Try a Cloning Design… and open the exercise for joining two pieces with no scar. It opens the example sequences and places a brief in the agent panel. Sending the brief uses the AI agent; the manual cloning tools do not need it.

Read the privacy page before sending sequence information to an AI provider.
